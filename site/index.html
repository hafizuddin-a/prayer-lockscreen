Prayer Live Wallpaper

Privacy policy · Download the app

Last updated: 11 November 2026

Prayer Live Wallpaper shows prayer times on your lock screen: the official Singapore timetable for Singapore, and times calculated on your phone for other places. This page says what the app does with information. The short version: it does not collect any.

What the app collects

Nothing. The app has:

  • no accounts and no sign-in
  • no analytics and no crash-reporting service
  • no advertising and no ad identifiers
  • no contacts access and no storage permission

Location

The app has one optional use of your location, and you start it yourself. If you tap Use my location on the location screen, Android asks whether to allow approximate location (the app asks for nothing more precise). The app then reads your phone's approximate position once, on the phone, and picks the nearest city from a list that is built into the app. That is all it is used for.

  • The position is not saved. Only the name of the city you end up with is stored, on the phone.
  • It is not sent anywhere. The calculation of prayer times for a place happens on the phone, from the city's coordinates, with no network request.
  • The app never reads your location in the background, and never reads it unless you tap the button.
  • You do not need to use it. You can search for your city by name instead, and Singapore needs no location at all.

Reminders (optional)

Reminders are off until you turn them on. When you do, Android asks whether the app may show notifications, and may take you to a settings screen to allow exact alarms (so reminders arrive at the exact minute). The app uses the notification permission for prayer reminders and for nothing else.

  • The alarms are set on your phone with Android's alarm service, and the notifications are shown by your phone. No server is involved and nothing is sent anywhere.
  • To measure whether reminders arrive on time, the app keeps a small log for 14 days: the time each reminder was planned for, the time it actually arrived, and what happened to it (shown, blocked because notifications were off, too late, or missed). It holds no personal information. It stays on the phone, in the app's private storage, and leaves it only if you tap Share history as text and choose where to send it.
  • Turning reminders off cancels the alarm. Clearing the app's storage deletes the log.

Home screen widgets (optional)

The app offers three home screen widgets. They show the prayer times, the Hijri date and a countdown, and they use only information that is already on the phone: the cached timetable, your settings, and the colours Android reports for your wallpaper (to choose light or dark text; the image itself is never read). They make no network request, ask for no permission, and send nothing anywhere. To keep them current the app sets two alarms at a time with Android's alarm service, and it keeps a small log for 14 days of when each widget was last redrawn (the time of each prayer or midnight, and the time the widgets were redrawn). It holds no personal information, stays on the phone in the app's private storage, and leaves it only if you tap Share as text on the widget health screen and choose where to send it.

What the app downloads

Only for Singapore. The app downloads the public Singapore prayer timetable from data.gov.sg, the Singapore government's open data portal, over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection. The timetable is published by Majlis Ugama Islam Singapura (MUIS) under the Singapore Open Data Licence.

The request asks for the public timetable only. It contains nothing about you. Like any internet request, it is seen by the server along with your network address (IP address). The app does not send any identifier, account or setting of yours, and it does not store the server's response beyond the timetable itself.

For every other place the app makes no network request for prayer times at all. Moving away from Singapore stops the daily download. The version from GitHub also makes the update requests described next.

App updates (the version from GitHub)

The app you download from GitHub can look for its own new versions. This is on by default and you can turn it off. A version installed from Google Play has none of this: Google Play does the updating.

  • When Keep the app up to date (under About) is on, the app asks GitHub for the newest release when you open it, at most every six hours, and about once a day in the background. When it is off, the app only asks when you tap Check for updates.
  • The request goes to api.github.com over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection and contains nothing about you. Like any internet request, it is seen by the server along with your network address (IP address) and the app's name and version (for example PrayerLiveWallpaper/1.8.1). The app sends no identifier, account or setting of yours.
  • If there is a newer version, the app downloads the file from GitHub and asks Android to install it. Before that, it checks that the file is this app, is newer, and is signed with the same key as the copy you have. Android checks the key again and has the last word: it may ask you to confirm.
  • For this the app asks Android for the permission to install apps, which you switch on under Allow from this source. The app uses it only for its own updates.
  • If notifications are allowed, the app may show one notification when a new version is ready. If not, you only see an Update button in the app.

What stays on your device

  • For Singapore, the timetable it downloaded, so the wallpaper works without a network.
  • The city you chose, and your choices for it: calculation method, Asr rule, any minutes you added or took away, and which prayer names to show.
  • The look you chose: background type, colours, dimming and the list position.
  • Your reminder choices (which prayers, a heads-up, silent or not) and the 14 day reminder log described above.
  • In the version from GitHub: whether automatic updates are on, when the app last checked for one, and the newest version it saw. A downloaded update file stays in the app's private storage until it is installed, and is removed the next time the app starts.
  • If you pick a photo, a smaller copy of it. It is chosen with the Android system photo picker, so the app only ever sees the one photo you select and never your gallery.
  • If you tap "Capture my wallpaper colours" (under Look, Background), or switch on "Match my current lock screen's colours" during setup, three colours (just numbers) read from your current wallpaper. The wallpaper image itself is never read or stored.

All of this lives in the app's private storage. It is not uploaded, not shared, and not included in cloud backups. Removing the app or clearing its storage deletes it.

Links

A few buttons open a link in your browser or another app: the data licence, the credits (GeoNames, CC BY 4.0) and Talk to the Developer, which opens the developer's Instagram page (@madebyhafiz). They open only when you tap them. The app itself sends nothing; what Instagram or your browser knows about you is covered by their own policies.
